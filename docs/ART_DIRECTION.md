# Art Direction and Visual Fidelity

## Primary objective
Reproduce the present-day garden as observed, not an idealized or generalized Jiangnan garden.

## Visual priorities
1. Spatial composition: water, paths, buildings, rockeries, walls, and vegetation must occupy the correct relationships.
2. Architectural truth: roof profile, eaves, columns, beams, doors, windows, railings, paving, and ornament must follow evidence.
3. Natural forms: tree silhouettes, trunk placement, bamboo density, shrubs, lotus, and rock contours should be asset-specific where visible.
4. Material response: plaster, aged timber, dark roof tiles, stone, brick, water, and foliage require physically plausible roughness, color, and surface variation.
5. View-dependent composition: framed views, occlusion, borrowed scenery, and transitions between enclosed and open spaces are part of the reconstruction.

## Avoid
- Generic "Chinese temple" or fantasy architecture.
- Exaggerated curved roofs not supported by references.
- Uniformly saturated greens or cyan water.
- Repeating the same tree or rock silhouette in prominent locations.
- Symmetry introduced for convenience.
- Decorative lanterns, bridges, walls, or pavilions that are not evidenced.
- AI-generated image details treated as factual reference.

## Reference comparison
For each important viewpoint, compare the render with a dated reference photograph from approximately the same camera position, direction, focal length, season, and time of day where possible. Record mismatches rather than hiding them through camera changes.

## Lighting baseline
The present-day garden changes with season, weather, and time. P0 uses neutral diagnostic lighting. Later reviews will define named capture conditions based on actual reference captures.
